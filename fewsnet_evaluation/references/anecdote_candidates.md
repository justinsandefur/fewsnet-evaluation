# Opening anecdote: candidates and fact-check

Compiled 15 Oct 2026. Tags: [V-full] = read in the primary document; [V-sum] = read via a fetch summary of the primary page; [S] = secondary only; [UNV] = could not verify.

**Recommendation: northeast Nigeria (Borno), 2016.** It fits the brief best. Before access opened, the area-level classification by both FEWS NET and the Cadre Harmonisé (CH) was Crisis (Phase 3). Bama town was opened in June 2016. Field teams found mass graves and famine-level conditions there, and a statistical reconstruction now puts excess deaths at about 490,000. Two caveats are needed, set out in §1.6: the CH did count *people* in Phase 4/5 even though no *area* was classified that high, and FEWS NET moved quickly to a famine warning once access opened in July 2016.

---

## 1. Northeast Nigeria 2015–17

### 1.1 What the classifications said before access opened

**Cadre Harmonisé, Nov 2015 (Oct–Dec 2015 current; published 5 Nov 2015).** [V-full, via Maxwell et al. 2018, pp. 13–14 and 40]
- This was the first CH analysis ever run in Nigeria, covering 8 states. "No areas were classified as Phase 4 (Emergency) or Phase 5 (Famine)" (Maxwell et al. 2018, p. 13).
- Borno and Yobe were classified at area level as Phase 3 (Crisis).
- Population counts nonetheless included 820,365 people in Phase 4 and 54,242 in Phase 5, all of the Phase 5 people in Borno.
- Phase 3+ in the three states was 3,966,810 (Table 1, p. 14).
- The timeline (p. 40) paraphrases the CH: 875,000 people in "emergency and famine situations" in Yobe and Borno.
- Source document: CILSS (2015), http://www.fao.org/fileadmin/user_upload/emergencies/docs/Fiche-Nigeria_Vfinal.pdf [not opened].

**Cadre Harmonisé, Mar 2016 (Mar–May 2016 current; published 4 Mar 2016).** [V-full, via Maxwell et al. 2018, pp. 14, 23, 41]
- No zone was classified Phase 4 or 5. "no specific zone/area had the threshold number of households (20 percent) in famine or emergency phases required for classification" (p. 41).
- About 216,969 people in Borno were counted in Phase 4. Zero were counted in Phase 5.
- Phase 3+ in the three states was **2,532,376**: Adamawa 81,953, Borno 1,627,266, Yobe 823,157 (Table 1).
- FEWS NET's July 2016 alert cites this round as estimating "more than three million people" in Phase 3+. That figure is probably the 8-state total and does not match the 3-state figure of 2.53m; flag this if the number is used.
- Source document: http://fscluster.org/sites/default/files/documents/fiche-nigeria_mar_2016_final_15mar.pdf [not opened; 403 to fetch].
- **Were inaccessible LGAs classified at all?** No LGA-level list was found for Mar 2016. The CH unit of analysis was mostly the senatorial zone until 2016, and became the LGA only "during the peak of the crisis in 2016" (Maxwell et al. 2018, p. 24). Inaccessible areas were usually given the phase of the accessible town in the LGA. Maxwell et al. say "This likely means that inaccessible areas are being under-classified" (p. 25). Respondents told them that only "Information from [the accessible areas] … makes it into the Cadre Harmonisé maps" (p. 27). OCHA's map of 25 Feb 2016 lists 11 Borno LGAs as inaccessible: Abadam, Bama, Dikwa, Guzamala, Gwoza, Kala Balge, Kukawa, Marte, Mobbar, Monguno, Ngala (https://reliefweb.int/map/nigeria/nigeria-borno-state-map-lga-accessibility-map-25022016-compared-25012015) [S, search summary].
- **The Bama data were thrown out.** Early 2016 screenings in Bama "showed a very high level of wasting and child mortality." They were judged low quality and "disqualified" from the CH. "By the time a proper assessment was mounted … the situation had improved dramatically" (Maxwell et al. 2018, pp. 23–24). [V-full]

**FEWS NET outlooks, Oct 2015 to May 2016.** [V-sum, fews.net pages]
- Oct 2015 Outlook (Oct 2015–Mar 2016): "Much of Borno, Yobe, and Adamawa … will be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3)" through March 2016. The outlook makes no mention of Emergency or Famine. https://fews.net/west-africa/nigeria/food-security-outlook/october-2015
- Feb 2016 Outlook (Feb–Sep 2016): poor households in the worst-affected areas of Borno, Yobe and Adamawa "will face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity between February and September 2016". The outlook makes no mention of Famine or Bama. https://fews.net/west-africa/nigeria/food-security-outlook/february-2016
- Mar 2016 Key Message Update: worst-affected households in most of Borno will "remain in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) through at least September". https://fews.net/west-africa/nigeria/key-message-update/march-2016
- May 2016 Key Message Update: most conflict-affected households "will remain in Crisis (IPC Phase 3)" through at least September. Neither Emergency nor Famine is mentioned. https://fews.net/west-africa/nigeria/key-message-update/may-2016
- Earlier history: on 7 Jan 2015 FEWS NET issued an alert that more than 3 million people in the northeast faced Phase 3 (Maxwell et al. 2018, p. 39). [S]

### 1.2 Access opens: Bama, June 2016

**MSF press release, 22 June 2016.** MSF visited on 21 June. https://www.msf.org/nigeria-least-24000-displaced-people-dire-health-situation-bama [V-sum]
- The camp held 24,000 people on a hospital compound, 15,000 of them children and 4,500 under five.
- The team counted **1,233 graves** near the camp dug in the past year, **480 of them children's**.
- **At least 188 deaths since 23 May**, "almost six people per day", mainly from diarrhoea and malnutrition.
- Rapid MUAC screening of more than 800 children found **19% with SAM**.
- Press caution: some outlets turned the 480 child graves into "almost 500 children died" in a month. That is wrong; the graves accumulated over a year.

**FEWS NET Food Security Alert, 7 July 2016**, "Food insecurity reaches extreme level in pockets of Nigeria's Borno State". https://fews.net/sites/default/files/documents/reports/20160707%20NE%20Nigeria%20Alert.pdf [V-full]
- Five rapid missions visited Bama between 15 and 21 June. Of 2,531 children screened by UNICEF or MSF, 15% had SAM.
- CDR "may have surpassed 2/10,000/day over the past month".
- In Monguno, an ALIMA MUAC screening of 12,190 children found 32% GAM and 13% SAM.
- The alert states that Famine (Phase 5) "could be occurring in the worst affected and less accessible pockets". It concludes that "an Emergency (CH/IPC Phase 4) is occurring".
- **This is FEWS NET's first public mention of possible Famine in this crisis.** It came about two weeks after access opened.
- The FEWS NET June 2016 Outlook (June 2016–Jan 2017) has the same language. It projects Emergency for IDPs in worst-affected and limited-access areas of Borno. https://fews.net/west-africa/nigeria/food-security-outlook/june-2016 [V-sum]
- The Federal Government declared a nutrition emergency in Borno on 27 June 2016 (Maxwell et al. 2018, p. 41).

### 1.3 The retrospective famine finding (Dec 2016)

- **FEWS NET Special Report and Alert, 13 Dec 2016**, "A Famine likely occurred in Bama LGA and may be ongoing in inaccessible areas of Borno State". https://fews.net/west-africa/nigeria/alert/december-2016 ; https://fews.net/west-africa/nigeria/special-report/december-2016 [V-sum]
  - "at least 2,000 Famine-related deaths may have occurred in Bama LGA between January and September", many of them young children.
  - The finding rests on "a representative mortality survey". The page does not name who ran the survey and gives no CDR value [UNV: survey identity and CDR].
  - The report also says "no representative nutrition surveys have been conducted in this area during 2016."
- **IPC Special Alert on Borno State, 16 Dec 2016.** https://fews.net/sites/default/files/documents/reports/IPC_Special_Alert_Nigeria_Dec2016.pdf [V-full]
  - The IPC Emergency Review Committee endorsed the finding: "A Famine likely occurred in April-August in some IDP enclaves (Bama and Banki towns)".
  - It found an "elevated likelihood" that famine was ongoing in inaccessible areas.
  - It also noted that "an IPC Famine cannot be confirmed according to IPC guidelines".
  - The October 2016 CH (senatorial-zone level) projected Phase 5 in Borno rising from 55,000 to 115,000 people.
- The politics: FEWS NET was criticised for speaking "outside the consensus". The CH reached no consensus on Phase 5 numbers for 2016. One government message, as reported, was "There is no famine in Nigeria" (Maxwell et al. 2018, pp. 26, 29–30). [V-full]

### 1.4 Surveys after access

- **Leidman et al. 2017**, MMWR 66(48):1332–5 [V-sum, CDC page]. These were SMART-type cluster surveys in six domains of Borno and Yobe, in Oct–Nov 2016 and Feb–Mar 2017.
  - GAM (WHZ) "did not exceed the WHO emergency threshold of 15%" in either round. The range was 6.4–14.3%.
  - CDR rose in every domain between rounds. Central Borno went from 0.55 to **1.36**/10,000/day; central Yobe from 0.63 to 1.14; northern Yobe from 0.50 to 1.02.
  - U5DR reached up to **2.63** (northern Yobe) and 2.60 (central Borno) in Feb–Mar 2017.
  - These figures are from accessible areas only.
- UNICEF/Nutrition Surveillance (NFSS) Round 1 (Oct–Nov 2016, published 13 Feb 2017) cautioned that estimates "should be understood to be representative of accessible areas" (Maxwell et al. 2018, pp. 13, 16, 23). [V-full]
- ACF small-scale SMART surveys (Round 2, 2017) found results not statistically different from the domain estimates (Maxwell et al. 2018, p. 24). Separate ACF Bama numbers were not found [UNV].

### 1.5 Checchi, Jarvis, van Zandvoort & Warsame (2023), PNAS 120(30): e2217601120

[V-full, Europe PMC full text of PMC10372555, re-checked today]
- Abstract: "some 490,900 people died as a result of this crisis during 2016 to 2019".
- Excess deaths, Apr 2016–Dec 2019, under the most likely counterfactual: **490,900 (95% CI 377,500–599,500)**. **47% were children under 5** (230,800; CI 166,000–288,800).
- The sensitivity range runs from 85,400 (reasonable best-case counterfactual) to 551,000 (worst-case). The significance statement rounds this to "90,000 (best-case) to 550,000 (worst-case)".
- Total deaths were 908,100, 41% of them under 5.
- **Excess deaths by year (Table 4):**

  | Year | Excess deaths (95% CI) |
  |---|---|
  | 2016 (Apr–Dec only) | 160,000 (117,600–212,600) |
  | 2017 | 208,900 (156,900–269,200) |
  | 2018 | 82,300 |
  | 2019 | 38,900 |

  About **75% of the excess toll fell in Apr 2016–Dec 2017**.
- **Mean CDR:** 0.73 (2016), 0.71 (2017), 0.42 (2018), 0.32 (2019) per 10,000/day. The national projection was 0.34. Death rates were "highest in 2016 to 2017".
- **By state (excess, most likely):** Adamawa 196,900; **Borno 116,400**; Yobe 176,500. Under the best-case counterfactual, Borno is negative (−31,900).
  - *Caveat for the anecdote:* the modelled toll is not concentrated in Borno. Do not write "half a million died in Borno."
- Context sentence: "As of mid-2016, about 34% of the three states' population was projected to be in phases 3 to 5". The CH phase is **not** a predictor in the model.
- Bias: if half of under-5 deaths were missed, the median rises to about 800,000.
- Bib key already exists: `checchi2023nigeria`.

### 1.6 Caveats to state honestly

1. "No stern warning" is accurate at the **area** level only. Both CH rounds before June 2016 counted some *people* in Phase 4: 820k in Nov 2015 and 217k in Mar 2016. Nov 2015 also counted 54k in Phase 5. No *area* was mapped above Phase 3.
2. FEWS NET's own projections stayed at Crisis (Phase 3) through May 2016, partly because the worst areas could not be seen. Once access opened, FEWS NET flagged possible Famine within about two weeks (7 July 2016). The story is therefore about blindness from lack of access, not a warning that was ignored.
3. Checchi's 490,900 is model-based. It covers three states over 45 months, mostly through indirect mortality, and is most sensitive to the counterfactual. The DHS 2018 did not show elevated under-5 mortality, and the paper acknowledges this.
4. The "at least 2,000 deaths in Bama" figure is FEWS NET's estimate. The underlying survey was not identified [UNV].

## 2. Alternative candidate (a): Mogadishu IDP settlements, Somalia

**Verdict: does not fit.** Mogadishu IDPs were already classified Emergency or flagged for famine when high mortality was measured.

- **Gu 2017.** The FSNAU/FEWS NET Post-Gu technical release (31 Aug 2017) put most IDP settlements in Crisis or Emergency (Phase 3–4). CDR and/or U5DR "surpassed emergency thresholds in seven of the population groups surveyed". https://fsnau.org/downloads/FSNAU-FEWS-NET-2017-Post-Gu-Technical-Release-Final-31-Aug-2017.pdf [S]
  - The specific Mogadishu IDP CDR for Gu 2017 was not extracted [UNV].
  - By Deyr 2017, the Mogadishu IDP CDR was 0.5–<1 and GAM was 16.1% (FSNAU Nutrition Update, Nov 2017). [S]
  - The whole 2017 Somalia response came after the IPC's early-2017 famine-risk alerts, so warnings were stern.
- **Deyr 2022.** The IPC Famine Review Committee (2 Dec 2022) reviewed Mogadishu and Baidoa IDPs. It found no current Famine but **projected Famine / Risk of Famine for Mogadishu IDPs for Apr–Jun 2023**. https://fsnau.org/downloads/IPC-Famine-Review-Report-Somalia_Dec2022.pdf [S]
  - From our own notes (lit_phase_outcomes.md): Baidoa IDPs in Oct 2022 had CDR 1.84 and U5DR 4.86, classified Phase 4.
  - The claim "Mogadishu CDR ~1.5, U5DR >4" was **not verified** against the FRC tables [UNV].
- Somalia remains a good *secondary* example of classification understating measured mortality (Phase 4 with CDR >2 in Bay, 2017), but not of "no warning".

## 3. Alternative candidate (b): South Sudan 2013–18

**Verdict: does not fit.** Stern warnings existed throughout.

- Checchi et al. (LSHTM, Sept 2018), *Estimates of crisis-attributable mortality in South Sudan, December 2013–April 2018*: **382,900 excess deaths**, about half of them violent. The toll peaked in 2016–17. Under-5 death rates were not clearly elevated. https://www.lshtm.ac.uk/south-sudan-full-report [S, press release and AP]
- IPC put large areas in Emergency from 2014 onward and **declared Famine in Leer and Mayendit in Feb 2017**. A high toll with stern warnings undercuts the point the anecdote is meant to make.
- Brief notes on the others (not researched in depth):
  - **Tigray 2021:** the IPC flagged about 350k people in Phase 5 in June 2021, so warnings were stern.
  - **Djibo 2022–23:** the CH classified Phase 5 populations, so warnings were stern.
  - **Kasai 2016–17:** possibly under-warned, but no population mortality estimate comparable to Checchi was found.
  - All three [UNV].

## 4. Draft opening paragraph (about 120 words)

> In February 2016, FEWS NET projected that the worst-affected households in northeast Nigeria would face Crisis (Phase 3) conditions through September, and the Cadre Harmonisé released in March classified no area of Borno, Yobe or Adamawa as Emergency or Famine (FEWS NET 2016a; Maxwell et al. 2018). Much of Borno could not be reached. When the army retook the town of Bama, an MSF team visiting on 21 June counted 1,233 graves dug over the previous year, 480 of them for children, and recorded almost six deaths a day in a camp of 24,000 (MSF 2016). An IPC review later concluded that famine had likely occurred there between April and August (IPC 2016). A statistical reconstruction now puts excess deaths in the three states at about 490,000 between April 2016 and December 2019, nearly half of them children under five (Checchi et al. 2023).

About 135 words. To reach 120, drop "Much of Borno could not be reached" or shorten the final sentence. An optional extra clause: "three-quarters of them by the end of 2017."

## 6. Could not verify

- The text of the Mar 2016 and Nov 2015 CH fiches (403 on fetch). Phases and numbers above come from the transcription in Maxwell et al. (2018), which is reliable but secondary. No LGA-by-LGA list of unanalysed areas was found for Mar 2016.
- Who ran the Bama "representative mortality survey" cited by FEWS NET in Dec 2016, and its CDR.
- The 3.0m vs 2.53m discrepancy for Phase 3+ in the Mar 2016 CH (probably 8 states vs 3 states).
- Mogadishu IDP CDR/U5DR values for Gu 2017 and Deyr 2022.
- Kasai, Tigray and Djibo details; the LSHTM South Sudan co-authors.
