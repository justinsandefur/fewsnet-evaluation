## 1. How food-crisis classifications are produced

**IPC Technical Manual v3.1 (IPC Global Partners, 2021).** https://www.ipcinfo.org/ipcinfo-website/featured-stories/news-details/en/c/1155156/ ; manual PDF: https://fscluster.org/sites/default/files/documents/ipc_technical_manual_3_final.pdf
The IPC classifies areas on a five-phase scale (1 Minimal to 5 Catastrophe/Famine) through a consensus analysis by country Technical Working Groups. An area is classed as **Famine** when three thresholds are all met: at least 20% of households face extreme food gaps, at least 30% of children aged 6–59 months are acutely malnourished (GAM by weight-for-height z-score), and the crude death rate exceeds 2 per 10,000 per day. [V] v3.1 created two levels of certainty:
- "Famine with solid evidence": all three thresholds are directly evidenced.
- "Famine with reasonable evidence": two thresholds are directly evidenced, and analysts infer the third.
Each piece of evidence gets a reliability score: R2 (reliable), R1+ or R1− (somewhat reliable, meaning weak on method and/or timeliness), or R0 (usable only under stated conditions). Analyses must meet minimum evidence levels, and there are special protocols for areas with limited or no humanitarian access. [V] Protocol page: https://www.ipcinfo.org/ipc-manual-interactive/ipc-chronic-food-insecurity-protocols/function-2-classify-severity-and-identify-key-drivers/protocol-24-evaluate-evidence-reliability/en/

**IPC Famine Fact Sheet / Guidance Note on Famine.** https://www.ipcinfo.org/fileadmin/user_upload/ipcinfo/docs/IPC_Famine_Factsheet.pdf ; https://www.ipcinfo.org/fileadmin/user_upload/ipcinfo/docs/IPC-Guidance-Note-on-Famine.pdf
Famine classifications, and any analysis where the technical working group cannot agree, go to an independent **Famine Review Committee (FRC)**. The FRC can say a Famine classification is "plausible", "confirmed", or not supported. The factsheet gives the mortality threshold as "2 or 4" per 10,000 per day. The 4 is the under-five death rate. [V]

**Cadre Harmonisé (CILSS / ECOWAS / UEMOA).** https://www.ipcinfo.org/ch/ ; Food Security Cluster handbook: https://handbook.fscluster.org/docs/672-cadre-harmonis%C3%A9
The Cadre Harmonisé is the IPC-equivalent consensus system used in the Sahel and West Africa, with CILSS coordinating. It runs two cycles a year, in Oct/Nov and Feb/Mar, uses the same five phases, and its results enter the GRFC as "IPC/CH". [V] The March 2026 cycle projected more than 3 million people in Phase 4 for the June–Aug 2026 lean season. It also put about 15,000 people in Borno, Nigeria, at risk of Catastrophe (Phase 5), the first time in nearly a decade. [S] WFP press release: https://www.wfp.org/news/millions-central-sahel-and-nigeria-risk-food-cuts-world-food-programme-faces-severe-funding ; UN News Jan 2026: https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/01/1166776

**FEWS NET, *Scenario Development for Food Security Early Warning* (Guidance Document, 2018).** https://fews.net/sites/default/files/documents/reports/Guidance_Document_Scenario_Development_2018.pdf ; *Journalist's Guide* (Aug 2025): https://fews.net/sites/default/files/2025-08/FEWSNET_Journalists_Guide_2025.pdf
FEWS NET runs an eight-step scenario process every four months. Analysts set a baseline, make explicit assumptions about hazards and markets, project household food and income sources, and then classify the "most likely" outcome eight months ahead. The Food Security Outlook is published in Feb, Jun and Oct, with Outlook Updates in Apr, Aug and Nov. FEWS NET has offices in "about 20 presence countries" and covers other countries by remote monitoring. [V, from the guide text] Its classifications are "IPC-compatible": they follow key IPC protocols but are not consensus IPC products and "do not always match" the IPC. FEWS NET cannot declare Famine. It issues "Risk of Famine" when a credible alternative scenario, not the most likely one, would lead to Famine. [V]

**FSIN / Global Network Against Food Crises, *Global Report on Food Crises* (GRFC), annual.** 2026 edition: https://openknowledge.fao.org/handle/20.500.14283/cd9424en ; technical notes: https://www.fightfoodcrises.net/sites/default/files/resource/file/2026_GRFC_APP_TECHNOTES.pdf ; JRC summary: https://joint-research-centre.ec.europa.eu/jrc-news-and-updates/food-crises-no-respite-14-m-people-suffer-most-severe-level-food-insecurity-2025-2026-04-24_en
The GRFC compiles IPC, CH, FEWS NET and WFP CARI-type estimates for a preselected list of about 65 food-crisis countries. A country is included only if its data meet the GRFC's technical requirements. [V] Headline series (people in IPC/CH Phase 3+ or equivalent, by data year):

| Data year (report) | People in Phase 3+ | Countries with usable data | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2016 (GRFC 2017) | ~108 m | 48 | [U] |
| 2022 (GRFC 2023) | ~258 m | 58 | [S] |
| 2023 (GRFC 2024) | 281.6 m (21.5% of analysed population) | 59 | [V] |
| 2024 (GRFC 2025) | 295.3 m; 1.9 m in Phase 5 (record) | 53 of 65 | [V] |
| 2025 (GRFC 2026) | 266 m (22.9% of analysed population); 1.4 m in Phase 5 across 6 countries; 39 m+ in Phase 4 | 47 of 65 | [V] |

In GRFC 2026, 18 countries or territories lacked comparable data. The report calls this "the lowest availability of data in a decade" and blames funding cuts and conflict access. [V] Five of the missing cases (Burkina Faso, Republic of Congo, Ethiopia, refugees in Algeria, migrants in Ecuador) held about 27 million Phase 3+ people in 2024. **The fall from 295 m to 266 m therefore mostly reflects missing data, not improvement.** [V/S] GRFC 2025 also warned that humanitarian allocations to food sectors could fall by up to 45% in 2025. [S] IFPRI blog: https://www.ifpri.org/blog/2025-global-report-on-food-crises-rising-food-insecurity-waning-humanitarian-assistance/

**Lentz, E., Baylis, K. & Kim, C. (2025). "Official estimates of global food insecurity undercount acute hunger." *Nature Food* 6(12).** https://www.nature.com/articles/s43016-025-01267-z ; PMC: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12717001/
The authors re-examine more than 10,000 IPC area analyses (2017–2023, about 1 billion people). They find that area classification around the Phase 2/3 boundary misses about 1 in 5 acutely food-insecure people: 293.1 m in Phase 3+ against the IPC's 226.9 m, so about 66 m uncounted. The error runs against the common claim that the IPC inflates numbers. [S, via phys.org / PubMed] A Matters Arising reply is on SSRN (https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6468905). [U: content not checked]

## 2. Data gaps: coverage, access, government blocking, surveys, mortality

**Ethiopia: IPC suspended since 2021.** IPC May 2021 Tigray analysis: https://www.ipcinfo.org/fileadmin/user_upload/ipcinfo/docs/IPC_Ethiopia_Acute_Food_Insecurity_2021MaySept_national.pdf ; FAO IPC evaluation (2022): https://openknowledge.fao.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/0895bc1b-af40-434d-866b-56d80230f70d/content ; Devex on Lowcock: https://www.devex.com/news/mark-lowcock-ethiopian-government-blocked-tigray-famine-declaration-103410
The June 2021 IPC update put about 401,000 people in Tigray and neighbouring zones in Phase 5 (Catastrophe). The Government of Ethiopia refused to endorse it. The IPC Global Steering Committee published anyway and triggered an FRC. The FAO evaluation states that no IPC analysis has been carried out in Ethiopia since and that the IPC is "suspended" there. [S] Mark Lowcock says the government was "quite effective in slowing down" the declaration system. [S] Ethiopia is missing from GRFC 2026 because permissions and funding for data collection were lacking. [S] https://borkena.com/2026/07/06/ethiopia-excluded-from-2026-global-food-crisis-report-due-to-data-gaps-amid-rising-humanitarian-needs/ OCHA's 2026 open-data report leaves Ethiopia, Palestine, Myanmar and Venezuela out of its humanitarian-needs availability metric because the data is "not produced or too sensitive to share". [V]

**Sudan: government withdrew from the IPC on 23 Dec 2024.** Dabanga/ReliefWeb: https://reliefweb.int/report/sudan/famine-declared-five-areas-sudan-govt-withdraws-ipc ; FRC Dec 2024: https://reliefweb.int/report/sudan/famine-review-committee-sudan-december-2024-conclusions-and-recommendations
The army-aligned government's agriculture minister announced the withdrawal the day before the FRC published. The government called the analysis speculative and short of field data. The FRC went on to find Famine in five areas, including Zamzam, Abu Shouk and Al Salam camps and parts of the Western Nuba Mountains. [S] The IPC has kept analysing Sudan through the Global Support Unit and the FRC.

**Sudan famine determinations.** FRC July 2024 (published 1 Aug 2024): https://www.ipcinfo.org/fileadmin/user_upload/ipcinfo/docs/IPC_Famine_Review_Committee_Report_Sudan_July2024.pdf ; FRC Oct/Nov 2025: https://www.ipcinfo.org/fileadmin/user_upload/ipcinfo/docs/IPC_Famine_Review_Committee_Report_Sudan_Oct_2025.pdf
- **Aug 2024:** the FRC found Famine "plausible" in Zamzam camp, which held more than 400,000 displaced people. This was the first FRC famine determination in more than 7 years and only the third since the IPC was created. [S] It relied on "reasonable evidence" because access for surveys was limited.
- **Nov 2025:** the FRC confirmed Famine (reasonable evidence) in El Fasher and Kadugli for Sep 2025–Jan 2026, with about 375,000 people in Phase 5 and 20 more areas at risk. [S] https://www.ipcinfo.org/ipcinfo-website/countries-in-focus-archive/issue-137/en/
- The CGD blog (below) reports that fewer than half of Sudan's SMART surveys planned for 2025 took place. [S]

**Gaza: Famine confirmed Aug 2025, lifted Dec 2025.** IPC special snapshot (Aug 2025): https://www.ipcinfo.org/ipcinfo-website/countries-in-focus-archive/issue-134/en/ ; WHO release: https://www.who.int/news/item/22-08-2025-famine-confirmed-for-first-time-in-gaza ; IPC response to critics: https://www.ipcinfo.org/fileadmin/user_upload/ipcinfo/docs/Response_to_Issues_Raised_Following_IPC_Famine_Classification_in_the_Gaza_Strip.pdf ; Dec 2025 update: https://www.un.org/unispal/document/un-agencies-press-release-19dec25/
- **Aug 2025:** on 22 Aug the FRC confirmed Famine in Gaza Governorate, the first famine in the Middle East. More than 500,000 people were in Phase 5, with 641,000 projected by the end of September and Famine expected to spread to Deir al-Balah and Khan Younis. [V/S] Whether this was solid or reasonable evidence, and how malnutrition was measured (MUAC-based), should be checked in the FRC report. [U]
- **Dec 2025:** after the October 2025 ceasefire and more deliveries, the IPC found no area in Famine. [V]
- **The dispute:** the Israeli government and some think-tanks (FDD, NCRI) contested the classification. https://govextra.gov.il/mda/ipc/gaza/ This is the most prominent live example of arguments over the politics of famine numbers.

**Yemen: access collapse.** HRW (Jan 2026): https://www.hrw.org/news/2026/01/08/houthi-detentions-halting-aid-in-crisis-hit-yemen ; FDD (Jan 2026): https://www.fdd.org/analysis/2026/01/29/houthi-actions-force-world-food-programme-to-close-operations-in-northern-yemen/
By Dec 2025 the Houthis held 69 UN staff. The UN suspended operations in Sa'ada, and in late Jan 2026 WFP began closing its operations in northern Yemen. Food-security and nutrition programmes in the monitored areas fell from 15 organisations across 14 districts (Jul 2024) to 2 organisations in 3 districts (Jul 2025). [S] Primary data collection in Houthi-controlled Yemen is now minimal. [S/U]

**DHS Program: terminated Feb 2025, partially revived 2026.** Devex: https://www.devex.com/news/demographic-and-health-surveys-reemerge-with-gates-funds-after-trump-cut-111840 ; Data Rescue Project (30 Jan 2026): https://www.datarescueproject.org/demographic-and-health-surveys/ ; UN DESA impact paper: https://www.un.org/development/desa/pd/sites/www.un.org.development.desa.pd/files/undesa_pd_2025_technical-paper_dhs-impact.pdf ; PNAS (2026): https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2513242123
In Feb 2025 USAID ended ICF's $236.8 m DHS-8 contract, which was meant to run 2024–2029. More than two dozen surveys in the field stopped. [S] On 23 Jan 2026 the programme was continued under an emergency three-year Gates Foundation grant. Data access has resumed through the DHS site and IPUMS. [S] About 15 surveys are listed as ongoing, against about 25 at shutdown. [S] **Status as of Sept 2026:** operating on bridge funding with no long-term financing. [S] DHS matters here because it supplies the anthropometric and mortality baselines that IPC analyses depend on.

**SMART and nutrition surveillance.** Action Against Hunger (2025), *No data, no response*, summarised in ENN Field Exchange 77: https://www.ennonline.net/fex/77/en/lack-nutrition-data-and-its-impact-response-capability
Funding cuts have suspended surveys and weakened surveillance in Mali, Niger, Sudan and elsewhere in West and Central Africa. In some countries, 2024 data were used to project 2025 needs. [S] I did not find a global count of SMART surveys per year. [U: a gap to fill, possibly from the SMART/ACF survey database]

**Checchi, F., Warsame, A., Treacy-Wong, V., Polonsky, J., van Ommeren, M. & Prudhon, C. (2017). "Public health information in crisis-affected populations: a review of methods and their use for advocacy and action." *The Lancet* 390(10109): 2297–2313.** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28602558/
The authors checked a minimal essential set of public-health information, including mortality and nutrition, in large conflict and disaster crises since 2010. That information was available and timely "only in a small minority of cases". [S] This is the core citation for the point that outcomes in crises are rarely measured.

**Checchi, F. et al. (2022). "A method for small-area estimation of population mortality in settings affected by crises." *Population Health Metrics* 20.** https://pophealthmetrics.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12963-022-00283-6
The paper models excess mortality by district and month because vital registration and surveillance are largely absent. [V]

**Checchi, F. & Robinson, W.C. (2013). *Mortality among populations of southern and central Somalia affected by severe food insecurity and famine during 2010–2012.* FAO/FSNAU and FEWS NET.** https://fsnau.org/downloads/Somalia_Mortality_Estimates_Final_Report_8May2013_upload.pdf
About 258,000 excess deaths between Oct 2010 and Apr 2012, including 133,000 children under five: 4.6% of the population and 10% of under-fives. Deaths peaked at about 30,000 a month in May–Aug 2011. Warnings began in Aug 2010, about 11 months before the 20 Jul 2011 declaration. [S] This is the canonical case of an accurate forecast with no timely response.

**Palmer, J., Alhaffar, M., Garba, B., Hassan, J., Ratnayake, R., Seal, A., Jelle, M. & Checchi, F. (2025). "The politics and challenges of measuring mortality for humanitarian decision-making in Somalia, the Central African Republic and Bangladesh." medRxiv preprint.** https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.06.19.25329924v1
Based on 23 key-informant interviews. Insecurity, bureaucratic obstacles and (in CAR) censorship leave mortality data gaps outside government-controlled areas. [S]

## 3. The 2025 US aid cuts and humanitarian funding

**FEWS NET shutdown and restart.** FEWS NET relaunch notice: https://fews.net/fews-net-relaunches-website-resumes-global-food-security-analysis ; Devex: https://www.devex.com/news/fews-net-once-usaid-s-flagship-famine-warning-system-is-back-online-110357 ; NPR (Aug 2025): https://www.npr.org/2025/08/11/nx-s1-5475193/u-s-famine-warning-system-comes-back-but-questions-loom ; NPR (1 Sep 2026): https://www.npr.org/2026/09/01/nx-s1-5944472/famine-usaid-fews-net ; CGD: https://www.cgdev.org/blog/famine-early-warning-system-back-online-its-warnings-are-dire
- **2025:** FEWS NET went offline in late Jan 2025 under the foreign-aid freeze. One report appeared in May, and parts of the work resumed from mid-April. The website relaunched on 24–25 Jun 2025 with a Global Food Security Update running through Sept 2025. [S] Chemonics and AIR have implemented it historically. [S]
- **2026:** the State Department now oversees FEWS NET. In Aug 2026 it paused reporting on Somalia, Afghanistan and Yemen, saying coverage follows "need, policy priorities, access, cost-efficiency". After public pressure it announced in Sept 2026 that reporting would resume. [S, NPR/KMUW] Somalia's FEWS NET profile had no update after July 2026. [S] US humanitarian aid to Somalia fell from about $770 m to near zero, and WFP's monthly caseload there fell from about 2 m to 350,000. [S]

**USAID dissolution.** CRS: https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/IF/PDF/IF10261/IF10261.20.pdf ; Donor Tracker: https://donortracker.org/policy_updates?policy=us-government-announces-official-closure-of-usaid-2025

## 4. The forecasting landscape

**Andrée, B.P.J., Chamorro, A., Kraay, A., Spencer, P. & Wang, D. (2020). "Predicting Food Crises." World Bank Policy Research Working Paper 9412.** https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/304451600783424495/pdf/Predicting-Food-Crises.pdf
This is the statistical backbone of the World Bank's Famine Action Mechanism. Random-forest and logit models on FEWS NET IPC-compatible outcomes predict transitions into crisis with useful lead times. Structural and conflict variables carry most of the predictive power. [V: abstract] A companion paper is WPS 9413, "Stochastic Modeling of Food Insecurity" (Wang, Andrée, Chamorro, Spencer), https://ideas.repec.org/p/wbk/wbrwps/9413.html

**World Bank Famine Action Mechanism (FAM), launched 2018** with the UN, ICRC, Google, Microsoft and AWS. https://www.worldbank.org/en/programs/famine-early-action-mechanism
FAM's goal is to link warning triggers to pre-arranged finance. It cites savings of $2.8 per $1 from early action. [S] Its current operational status, including whether any FAM-branded trigger has fired, is not clear from public sources. [U]

**Balashankar, A., Subramanian, L. & Fraiberger, S.P. (2023). "Predicting food crises using news streams." *Science Advances* 9.** [U: author list and volume from memory] https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.abm3449
Text features from news improve IPC-phase forecasts up to 12 months ahead compared with models without them. [S]

**Constenla-Villoslada, S., Liu, Y., McBride, L., Ouma, C., Mutanda, N. & Barrett, C.B. (2025). "High-frequency monitoring enables machine learning–based forecasting of acute child malnutrition for early warning." *PNAS* 122(23).** https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2416161122
Using northern Kenya sentinel data, the authors show that updating training data with high-frequency measurement of the target variable improves ML forecasts of child wasting. The implication is that forecasting depends on continued outcome measurement. [S]

**WFP HungerMap LIVE.** https://hungermap.wfp.org/ ; Foini et al. (2023/24), "Forecasting trends in food security with real time data," *Communications Earth & Environment*: https://www.nature.com/articles/s43247-024-01698-9
HungerMap covers about 90–95 countries. It combines mVAM phone surveys, prices, rainfall and NDVI, and conflict data. An XGBoost nowcast estimates food-consumption prevalence where no survey exists, and a short-horizon forecast layer sits on top. [S] The CGD blog reports WFP remote-monitoring country offices falling from 35 (2022) to 18 (2025) and interviews from 1.1 m (2024) to 800,000 (2025). [S] **Because the nowcast is trained on WFP's own survey data, those cuts weaken it.**

**FAO–WFP *Hunger Hotspots* (twice a year).** June 2026 edition: https://www.fao.org/newsroom/detail/new-fao-wfp-report-warns-worsening-hunger-puts-13-hotspots-at-significant-risk/en ; series: https://www.wfp.org/publications/hunger-hotspots-fao-wfp-early-warnings-acute-food-insecurity
This is a qualitative, analyst-driven outlook for the next 5–6 months. The Jun–Nov 2026 edition names 13 hotspots:
- Highest concern: Sudan, South Sudan, Yemen, Palestine, northeast Nigeria, and Somalia (risk of Famine in Burhakaba, Bay).
- Very high concern: Afghanistan and DRC, among others.
- Conflict is a driver in 12 of the 13. [S]

**OCHA anticipatory action / CERF.** https://cerf.un.org/anticipatory-action ; CERF Annual Results Report 2025: https://cerf.un.org/sites/default/files/resources/CERF_ARR_2025.pdf ; Centre for Humanitarian Data: https://centre.humdata.org/how-ochas-anticipatory-approach-to-predictable-shocks-is-speeding-up-post-disaster-response/
Each framework has three parts: a predictive model or trigger, pre-arranged finance, and a pre-agreed action plan. By end-2025 CERF had pre-arranged $137.7 m for 26 frameworks covering 4.4 m people in 21 countries. [S, citing CERF ARR 2025] Examples include $10 m for Ethiopia drought (Sept 2024) and $4 m released two days before Cyclone Chido made landfall in Mozambique. [S] Most triggers are hydromet (drought, flood, cyclone). **Conflict-driven food crises, which is where famines now occur, are largely outside the trigger model.** [my inference]

**OCHA Centre for Humanitarian Data: predictive analytics and model peer review.** https://centre.humdata.org/the-centres-predictive-analytics-focus-models-peer-review-community/
The Centre runs a peer-review framework for humanitarian models, including a review of ACLED CAST (https://data.humdata.org/dataset/2048a947-5714-4220-905b-e662cbcd14c8/resource/7faed1a0-9b5e-4c7e-890e-ab3f654a028d/download/model-report-acled-cast.pdf). [V: document exists]

**ACLED CAST (Conflict Alert System).** Methodology: https://acleddata.com/methodology/cast-methodology ; code: https://github.com/ACLED/cast-public
CAST forecasts counts of battles, explosions/remote violence and violence against civilians for each rolling four-week period, six periods ahead, at the admin-1 level for every country. It uses a LightGBM model with hierarchical reconciliation and publishes past-forecast accuracy metrics. [V] **International Crisis Group's *CrisisWatch*** (https://www.crisisgroup.org/crisiswatch) is a qualitative monthly monitor with "risk alerts" and no published skill scores. [U: not re-checked for 2026]

**FEWS NET accuracy evaluations**, the most directly relevant evidence for the concept note:
- Choularton, R.J. & Krishnamurthy, P.K. (2019), "How accurate is food security early warning? Evaluation of FEWS NET accuracy in Ethiopia," *Food Security* 11: 333–344. https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12571-019-00909-y Covers Ethiopia 2011–2017. Accuracy is high overall but lower in the food-insecure northeast, during the 2015/16 El Niño, and at transitions into crisis. [S]
- Backer, D. & Billing, T. (2021), "Validating Famine Early Warning Systems Network projections of food security in Africa, 2009–2020," *Global Food Security* 29: 100510. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2211912421000201 Covers 25 African countries at grid-cell level. Overall accuracy is 84%, but falls as severity rises:
  - Phase 1: at least 90%.
  - Phase 3: 60–70%.
  - Phase 4: about 40% in East and West Africa.
  - Errors lean toward over-projection.
  - By region: East Africa 75%, West 87%, Southern 86%. [S]
- Bertetti, M., Agnolucci, P., Calzadilla, A. & Capra, L. (2024), "An independent evaluation of the Famine Early Warning Systems Network food security projections," arXiv:2410.09384. https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.09384 Overall accuracy is 78% at admin–livelihood-zone level, with large variation by region and horizon. [V: abstract]
- **Caveat for all three:** the "ground truth" is FEWS NET's own later current-situation classification, not an independent measurement. This is central to the concept note.

**Performance debates.**
- **Gaza:** de Zwarte, I., de Waal, A. & Lumey, L.H. (2026), "Rethinking current famine classification: insights from history," *The Lancet* (17 Feb 2026). https://worldpeacefoundation.org/publication/rethinking-current-famine-classification-insights-from-history/ They argue that IPC mortality thresholds, built for rural African populations, need about a 20-fold mortality rise in a population with Gaza's demographic profile (against 6–7-fold in rural settings) before Famine is classified. As a result, about 95% of Gaza was in Phase 3+ from Dec 2023 to Oct 2025 while the Famine classification came late. They propose more sensitive signals such as birth outcomes and mortality among older children. [S, via WPF summary]
- **Sudan:** Zamzam (2024) and El Fasher/Kadugli (2025) were classified on "reasonable evidence" because sieges prevented mortality surveys.

## 5. Critiques: the politics of numbers and classification

**Howe, P. & Devereux, S. (2004). "Famine intensity and magnitude scales: a proposal for an instrumental definition of famine." *Disasters* 28(4): 353–372.** https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.0361-3666.2004.00263.x
The paper separates *intensity* (severity at a place and time) from *magnitude* (total mortality). This is the intellectual precursor of the IPC Phase 5 thresholds. [V]

**Maxwell, D. & Hailey, P. (2020). *The Politics of Information and Analysis in Famines and Extreme Emergencies: Synthesis of Findings from Six Case Studies.* Feinstein International Center / Centre for Humanitarian Change.** https://fic.tufts.edu/wp-content/uploads/PIA-Synthesis-Report_May-13.pdf
Case studies of Ethiopia, Kenya, Nigeria, Somalia, South Sudan and Yemen document governments, agencies and donors shaping access, data collection and the release of classifications. The authors' point is that analytical capacity has improved sharply, but political interference limits prevention. [V]

**Maxwell, D., Khalif, A., Hailey, P. & Checchi, F. (2020). "Viewpoint: Determining famine: Multi-dimensional analysis for the twenty-first century." *Food Policy* 92: 101832.** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0306919220300166
Threshold-based famine determination fails where data are missing or access is denied. The authors argue for multi-dimensional analysis that includes mortality modelling. [V]

**Maxwell, D., Day, M. & Hailey, P. (2023). *Do Famine Declarations Really Lead to Increased Funding?* Feinstein International Center brief.** https://fic.tufts.edu/wp-content/uploads/Famine-declarations-and-warnings_6-8.pdf
They find little evidence that declarations raise funding, with one major exception: in Somalia 2011, appeal contributions nearly doubled "overnight" after the declaration. In Somalia 2017 a *projection* triggered the scale-up. "On the whole, there doesn't appear to be much of an obvious relationship between either declarations or warnings and actual funding flows." [V: text extracted] **This bears directly on the question of whether aid follows forecasts.**

**Maxwell, D. (2019). *Famine Early Warning and Information Systems in Conflict Settings.* LSE Conflict Research Programme.** https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/102836/1/Maxwell_famine_early_warning_and_information_systems_published.pdf ; and Maxwell & Hailey, *Towards Anticipatory Information Systems and Action* (Feinstein). [V: document exists]

**de Waal, A. (2018). *Mass Starvation: The History and Future of Famine.* Polity.** This is the standard account of famine as political, and of starvation used as a weapon. [V: bibliographic]

**Darcy, J. & Hofmann, C.-A. (2003). *According to Need? Needs Assessment and Decision-Making in the Humanitarian Sector.* HPG Report 15, ODI.** https://odi.org/documents/2310/347.pdf

- Only 36% of aid recipients surveyed thought aid went to those who needed it most. [S]

## 7. Synthesis

**(a) Coverage.** Systematic *forecasting* reaches most of the humanitarian food-crisis caseload, but with only one method and one source of truth.
- **Forecast coverage:** in a typical recent year the GRFC's 65 crisis countries are almost all covered by at least one projection:
  - IPC or Cadre Harmonisé projections, about 6–8 months ahead;
  - FEWS NET outlooks, about 20 presence countries plus remote monitoring;
  - FAO–WFP Hunger Hotspots;
  - WFP HungerMap nowcasts, about 90 countries.
- **Usable-data coverage is shrinking:** 59 countries had usable data for 2023, 53 of 65 for 2024 and only 47 of 65 for 2025. The missing countries are not random. They include Ethiopia (no IPC since 2021), Burkina Faso and, intermittently, Myanmar and Venezuela, which alone held about 27 m Phase 3+ people when last measured.
- **Independent outcome measurement is much thinner:**
  - FEWS NET's and the IPC's "ground truth" is mostly their own later current-status classification.
  - Measurements independent of the classifiers, such as SMART anthropometry and mortality surveys, DHS and mortality surveillance, exist for only a minority of crisis area-periods. Checchi et al. (2017) found essential health information timely in only a small minority of major crises.
  - Rough estimate [my inference, not a published figure]: about 75–90% of the recognised caseload has a forecast, but perhaps a quarter to a third of crisis area-seasons have any contemporaneous survey-based measurement of malnutrition or mortality. Almost none has credible excess-mortality estimates in time to affect response.
- **The accuracy studies cannot answer the key question:** they report 78–84% overall accuracy, falling to about 40% at Phase 4 (Backer & Billing 2021). But they score forecasts against the same system's later classification, so they cannot show whether the classifications themselves track true deprivation. Lentz et al. (2025) suggest the IPC undercounts Phase 3+ by about 20%.

**(b) The biggest gaps.**
1. **Conflict and siege areas:** Sudan (Darfur, Kordofan), Gaza, northern Yemen and the central Sahel. These are exactly where famine now occurs, and there mortality thresholds can only be inferred ("reasonable evidence").
2. **Governments vetoing analysis:** Ethiopia since 2021, and Sudan's withdrawal in Dec 2024. The system has no answer when the host government refuses consent.
3. **Mortality:** almost no routine measurement. Declarations therefore lag. De Zwarte, de Waal and Lumey argue that the thresholds are also badly calibrated for non-rural populations.
4. **Sustained survey infrastructure:** DHS now runs on a three-year Gates bridge. WFP interviews fell about 27% and enumerator visits about a third. SMART surveys were skipped, for example most of those planned for Sudan in 2025.
5. **The response side:** FTS is voluntary and revised late. USAID's IATI and evaluation records have degraded. Maxwell, Day & Hailey find no systematic link between warnings or declarations and funding.

**(c) Why this matters now.**
- **Funding:** appeal funding fell from about $25 bn in 2024 to about $12–16 bn in 2025, depending on source. Appeals were cut to "hyper-prioritised" cores ($29 bn in 2025; $23 of $33 bn in 2026). That makes the ranking of crises by severity the main allocation tool, so errors or gaps in IPC and FEWS NET numbers now translate directly into who gets food.
- **Forecasting capacity is under the same pressure:** FEWS NET was dark for five months in 2025 and paused its Somalia, Afghanistan and Yemen coverage in Aug 2026 on "policy priorities". WFP lost about a third of its income, and OCHA, UNHCR and IOM cut data staff.
- **Headline trends mislead:** the GRFC's apparent 2025 "improvement" (295 m to 266 m) is mostly countries dropping out of the data. Donors reading headline trends risk mistaking fewer data for less hunger.
- **Weak feedback:** two famines were confirmed in the same year for the first time (Sudan, Gaza). The debates over Gaza and Zamzam show that without independent outcome data, contested classifications cannot be settled.
- **The argument for the note:** building independent outcome measurement (mortality, anthropometry) and credible out-of-sample evaluation of forecasts is cheap relative to the aid flows it steers. CGD estimates about $30 m a year for WFP's core data collection. It is also the missing link needed to test whether aid follows forecasts.
